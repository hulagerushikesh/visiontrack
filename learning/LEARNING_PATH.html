00

Orientation — the problem you're solving

Before any math: what "multi-object tracking" means, and the shape of the pipeline everything else plugs into.

What is tracking-by-detection?Detector finds boxes each frame; the tracker gives them stable IDs over time

What & why

A detector answers "where are the objects in this frame?" — it has no memory, so box #3 in frame 10 has no relationship to box #3 in frame 11. Tracking is the job of stitching those per-frame detections into persistent tracks, each with one identity that survives across frames, occlusions, and missed detections. "Tracking-by-detection" means you take detections as given and only solve the association problem — which is what this whole project is about.

The loop, once per frame

  • Predict — move each existing track forward with a motion model (Kalman).
  • Associate — match this frame's detections to predicted tracks (Hungarian on a cost matrix).
  • Update — correct matched tracks with their detection; start tracks for unmatched detections; age-out tracks that keep missing.

Geometry & detections

The primitives: how a box is represented, and how you measure whether two boxes are "the same place".

Bounding boxes, IoU & GIoUBox parametrizations and the overlap metric that drives motion association

What & why

A box can be written as corners (x1,y1,x2,y2), centre-size (x,y,w,h), or the Kalman-friendly (x,y,a,h) (centre, aspect-ratio, height). IoU (intersection over union) is the overlap score in [0,1] — the backbone of motion-only association. GIoU extends it so that non-overlapping boxes still get a meaningful gradient (plain IoU is 0 for any two disjoint boxes, which loses information about how far apart they are).

Learn

  • Why xyah? — aspect ratio + height change slowly and roughly independently, so they're better Kalman state variables than raw width/height.
  • IoU as a distance — association cost is usually 1 − IoU, so identical boxes cost 0.
  • GIoU's extra term — penalises the empty space in the smallest enclosing box.

02

State estimation — the Kalman filter

The mathematical heart. A Kalman filter is how each track "remembers" where it is and how fast it's moving, as a probability cloud it updates every frame. Spend the most time here.

Belief as a Gaussian: mean & covarianceA track's state isn't a point — it's a distribution with uncertainty

What & why

The filter never claims to know exactly where an object is. It carries a mean (best guess of state) and a covariance (how unsure it is, and how the variables correlate). Right after a detection the cloud is tight; after several missed frames it balloons. That growing-and-shrinking uncertainty is what later powers the gate and RQ3.

Learn

  • The 8-state vector — [x, y, a, h, vx, vy, va, vh]: position + its velocities (constant-velocity model).
  • Covariance intuition — the diagonal is per-variable variance; off-diagonals encode "if I'm wrong about x, I'm probably wrong about vx too".

Milestones

Predict & update — the two-step cycleMotion pushes the belief forward; a measurement pulls it back

What & why

Predict: apply the motion model (positions advance by their velocities), and grow the covariance by the process noise Q (the model isn't perfect). Update: when a detection arrives, blend prediction and measurement weighted by their relative certainties — the Kalman gain. Confident measurement + unsure prediction ⇒ trust the measurement, and vice-versa. This optimal blending is the Kalman filter.

Learn

  • Process noise Q vs measurement noise R — Q = "how wrong is my motion model", R = "how noisy is the detector". This repo scales both by object height (bigger/closer ⇒ more pixel noise).
  • The Kalman gain K — the ratio that decides who to trust; derive it once.
  • Joseph-form update — a rewrite of the covariance update that stays numerically stable (symmetric, positive-definite) instead of drifting — worth knowing why the "obvious" formula is avoided.

Milestones

Mahalanobis distance & gatingUncertainty-aware distance that rejects impossible matches before assignment

What & why

Euclidean distance treats every direction equally; Mahalanobis distance divides by the covariance, so a detection that's far in a confident direction is "expensive" but the same distance in an uncertain direction is cheap. A hard gate uses a χ² threshold on this distance to declare some track–detection pairs simply infeasible (never matched), shrinking the assignment problem and preventing absurd jumps.

Learn

  • Why χ² — a squared Mahalanobis distance of a Gaussian innovation is χ²-distributed, so the gate threshold is a p-value on "could this be the same object?".
  • Gate as feasibility, not ranking — remember this; it becomes the key fix in Stage 7/9.

03

Data association — the assignment problem

Given a cost for every track–detection pair, find the one-to-one matching with the lowest total cost. This is a classic combinatorial-optimization problem with an exact polynomial solution.

The Hungarian algorithmExact minimum-cost bipartite matching in O(n³)

What & why

You have a cost matrix (rows = tracks, cols = detections). A greedy "each track grabs its cheapest detection" can fail badly — two tracks may fight over one detection while a good global match goes unused. The Hungarian algorithm finds the provably optimal one-to-one assignment. This repo implements a rectangular O(n³) version with dual potentials (the algorithm's certificate of optimality), validated against SciPy.

Learn

  • Assignment problem — the formal statement (a permutation minimizing total cost).
  • Primal–dual intuition — potentials/labels that prove no cheaper matching exists; you don't need every detail, but grasp why duality gives optimality.
  • Rectangular case — #tracks ≠ #detections; handled by padding / allowing non-matches.
  • Alternatives — greedy and auction algorithms exist; know the trade-off (speed vs optimality).

04

Putting it together — ByteTrack & the track lifecycle

The orchestration: how a full tracker sequences association, decides when to trust weak detections, and manages tracks being born, confirmed, and killed.

ByteTrack two-stage associationMatch high-confidence detections first, then recover tracks with the leftover low-confidence ones

What & why

Most trackers throw away low-score detections. ByteTrack's insight: a low-score box is often a real object that's briefly occluded or blurred, not noise. So it associates in two rounds — high-score detections to tracks first, then a second round matching still-unmatched tracks against the low-score leftovers. This single idea recovers many would-be ID switches, which is why it's the baseline here.

Learn

  • Two-stage flow — round 1 (high) → round 2 (low, only for unmatched tracks) → create/kill.
  • The factored cost surface — cost.py assembles w_iou·motion ⊕ w_app·appearance ⊕ w_unc·uncertainty, all under the gate. Setting the extra weights to 0 recovers pure ByteTrack (bit-identical) — that's the ablation surface the whole study rides on.

Track lifecycle — the state machineTentative → Confirmed → Deleted, so a one-frame blip doesn't spawn a permanent ghost

What & why

A new detection could be a real object or a false positive. So tracks start Tentative, become Confirmed only after matching for N consecutive frames, and get Deleted after missing for M frames. This finite-state machine is what keeps the ID set clean — too eager and you get flicker-ghosts, too cautious and you drop real objects.

05

Evaluation & metrics

You can't improve what you can't measure. These are the standard MOT metrics — and understanding what each rewards is the difference between "looks better" and "is better".

CLEAR-MOT: MOTA, MOTP, IDSWThe classic detection-weighted metrics — and their blind spot

What & why

MOTA aggregates false positives, misses, and ID switches into one number; MOTP is localization precision; IDSW counts how often a track's identity jumps to a different object. Key limitation: MOTA is dominated by detection errors and barely reflects identity quality — a tracker can have great MOTA and still swap IDs constantly. That's the motivation for HOTA/IDF1.

HOTA & IDF1 — measuring identityAssociation-aware metrics; why HOTA is the modern standard

What & why

IDF1 measures how consistently each ground-truth identity is covered by a single track. HOTA explicitly factors performance into detection accuracy × association accuracy, so improvements in identity handling actually show up — which is exactly what appearance/motion tricks target. Your repo implements both from scratch and cross-checks them against trackeval to within 1e-3, so downstream numbers are trustworthy.

06

Statistical rigor — what makes it research

The line between "a tracker" and "a study". Every claim here is mean±std with a significance test, and every run is reproducible from its config hash.

Seeds, paired tests & effect sizeIs a +0.004 HOTA real, or noise? Significance testing answers it

What & why

A single number is a coin flip. This repo runs each configuration over multiple seeds/sequences and reports mean ± std. To compare a variant vs its baseline it uses paired tests (same sequences, same seeds) — a Wilcoxon signed-rank test and a paired bootstrap confidence interval, plus Cohen's d effect size. "Paired" is what gives the tiny-but-consistent appearance gains their statistical power.

Learn

  • p-value — probability of seeing this difference if the variant did nothing.
  • Why paired > unpaired — cancels per-sequence difficulty, so it detects small consistent effects (this is how 7→21 units flipped IDSW to p≈0.005).
  • Bootstrap CI — resample to get an interval without assuming normality.
  • Effect size — significance ≠ importance; report both.

RQ1 — Appearance re-ID (the main thesis)

The centerpiece question: when does adding a visual "what does this object look like?" signal help association, and when does it hurt? The answer here is the project's strongest result.

Embeddings, cosine cost & the EMA galleryTurn a crop into a vector; match by appearance similarity; remember it over time

What & why

An embedding maps an object crop to a vector where "same person" points the same way. Cosine distance between a detection's embedding and a track's stored appearance becomes an extra association cost. Because a single frame can be blurry, each track keeps an EMA gallery — an exponentially-weighted running average of its matched embeddings — so identity is remembered, DeepSORT-style. Your repo has both a hand-crafted colour histogram embedder (no model) and a deep OSNet ONNX re-ID model.

Learn

  • Hand-crafted vs learned — colour histograms are cheap but near a ceiling; deep re-ID (OSNet) roughly 2–4× the association gain.
  • EMA gallery — the momentum term trades stability vs adaptivity.
  • Compute-once caching — embeddings are the only GPU-ish step; precomputed to .npz so experiments stay CPU-fast.

The key insight: rank, don't vetoWhy appearance is robustly helpful-or-neutral — and never hurts here

What & why

The famous prediction was "appearance hurts on near-identical dancers (DanceTrack)". This project tested it on real DanceTrack and refuted it — appearance helped there too. The reason is the single most important design idea in the codebase: appearance is gated. The motion gate decides which pairs are feasible; the appearance term only ranks within that feasible set — it can never veto a good motion match. A bug where extra terms shrank the gate was fixed so each term also raises max_cost. "Appearance hurts" is a property of appearance-vetoing trackers, not of uniform-looking objects.

The evidence chain

  • MOT17 — deep re-ID cuts ID switches significantly (pooled 21 seq×detector units, p≈0.005).
  • Detector-quality gradient — inert on weak DPM (bad crops → useless embeddings); crop quality gates appearance, not scene ambiguity.
  • Synthetic probe — dial appearance diversity 0→1; benefit grows with distinctness, never flips negative.
  • DanceTrack — the hypothesis's own test case; appearance still helps.

RQ2 — Learned motion residual (honest negative)

Can a small neural net correct the constant-velocity Kalman prediction on non-linear motion? A neat result: it improves prediction in isolation but makes the tracker worse — and understanding why is the lesson.

A from-scratch MLP & the open-loop/closed-loop gapHand-written backprop; why a better predictor can be a worse tracker

What & why

The residual is a 2-layer tanh MLP that predicts a correction to the Kalman mean from the last few velocities — implemented in pure NumPy with hand-written backprop + Adam (matching the repo's from-scratch ethos; torch was installed but deliberately unused). It's a great chance to actually learn how a neural net trains under the hood.

The two-level result (learn this pattern)

  • Open-loop (fed clean ground-truth history): lowers next-centre error on DanceTrack −12.5% (non-linear) but +21.5% worse on near-linear MOT17. "Helps only non-linear motion" — confirmed.
  • Closed-loop (inside the tracker): hurts both. Trained on clean GT trajectories but fed the tracker's own noisy estimated history — a train/serve distribution shift — and it perturbs the association gate, creating switches.
  • Lesson — evaluating a component in isolation can lie; the system-level metric is what counts.

RQ3 — Calibrated uncertainty (honest negative)

Should you trust the Kalman filter's own covariance as a soft association weight, and recalibrate it if it's miscalibrated? The surprising answer: the "wrong" loose gate is actually a robustness feature.

Reliability, calibration & why tightening hurtsAre gated innovations really χ²? What happens if you force them to be

What & why

If the filter is well-calibrated, squared innovations should follow a χ²(4) distribution. Measuring this along GT trajectories showed the filter is badly under-confident — mean χ² ≈ 0.15 vs the expected 4 — so the Mahalanobis gate is effectively inert (this also explains an earlier null result). The tempting fix is to recalibrate (shrink the covariance so the gate is "correct"). Doing so under detector noise was catastrophic (HOTA −0.205, IDSW 40→402): a tight gate rejects noisy-but-correct detections.

Learn

  • Reliability diagram — plotting empirical vs expected coverage to judge calibration.
  • The counterintuitive conclusion — a loose gate is a robustness feature under noise; "textbook-correct" calibration is wrong for tracking-by-detection.

Datasets, caching & the platform

The engineering that makes a 3-dataset × N-seed × 3-thesis matrix tractable on a laptop with no GPU — plus the dataset and licensing realities.

MOT17, DanceTrack, the MOT format & licensingReal data, public detections, and why one dataset's frames can't ship

What & why

MOT17 = pedestrians with diverse appearance and public detections (DPM/FRCNN/SDP) so trackers compare without a detector confound. DanceTrack = near-identical outfits + non-linear motion (RQ1's hard case); it ships no detections, so this repo uses oracle-perturbed GT. Both use the same MOT text format (a shared parser). Licensing matters: MOT17 is CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial, incompatible with the repo's MIT), so real frames are gitignored and were purged from history; DanceTrack is CC-BY (permissive).

Compute-once caching & the from-scratch disciplineWhy the core stays NumPy-only and heavy deps stay lazy

What & why

Detection and embedding are the only expensive steps — so they run once per dataset and serialize to data/cache/. Every tracking experiment then reads cached (boxes, scores, embeddings, gt) and runs CPU-only in seconds. The architectural rule: core/ imports nothing but NumPy; scipy/torch/onnxruntime/matplotlib live in optional extras, lazily imported, never in the core. That discipline is what keeps the project installable and the "from-scratch" claim honest.

Where to take it next — redesign & advance

Once the above is solid, these are the real directions to make it more advanced. Each is a genuine research/engineering arc, roughly easiest → hardest.

Modern motion trackers: OC-SORT & BoT-SORTThe current strong baselines beyond vanilla ByteTrack

Direction

Implement OC-SORT (observation-centric re-update that fixes Kalman error accumulation during occlusion) and BoT-SORT (camera-motion compensation + a better appearance fusion). Both slot directly onto your existing cost surface and give a concrete "we improved the baseline" story with your own significance testing.

Real detector + more datasetsClose the biggest scope caveats in the current study

Direction

Two natural extensions already scoped in your docs: (1) run a real YOLOX ONNX detector on DanceTrack instead of oracle-perturbed GT — removes the "clean crops favour appearance" caveat on the marquee RQ1 result; (2) add SportsMOT as a second maneuver dataset to properly stress RQ2's motion residual. Both are pure additions to the loader/cache layer you already have.

Milestones

Learned & end-to-end trackingThe research frontier: transformers and graph-based association

Direction

The frontier moves association from hand-designed costs to learned ones: GNN association (nodes = detections/tracks, edges = match scores), and end-to-end transformer trackers (MOTR / TrackFormer) that jointly detect and track with attention. Ambitious, but your clean cost-surface + rigorous-eval harness is the ideal platform to measure whether they actually beat a well-tuned classical tracker — which is exactly the honest-measurement thesis, one level up.
