Computer Vision · junior → research roadmap

Learn computer vision in order — foundations to the research frontier

0% 0 / 0 milestones

A single ordered path through computer vision — from the math you can't skip to doing original research. The numbering is the order: each stage assumes the ones before it. Every stage carries a level badge (junior → mid → senior → research) so you always know where you are, and gives you what & why, the concepts to learn, something to build, canonical resources, and a checklist. Ticking boxes saves your progress in this browser.

Don't rush the tiers. Junior (00–02) is classical vision + the math; mid (03–09) is deep learning and the core tasks; senior (10–15) is modern architectures, generative & 3D, and production; research (16–18) is reading, reproducing, and producing new knowledge. Build a small project at the end of every tier — that's what turns "watched a course" into "can do the job".

00

Prerequisites — the math & code you can't skip

Junior

Everything downstream is linear algebra, calculus, and probability wearing a costume. Get comfortable here and the rest stops feeling like magic.

Linear algebra, calculus, probability & Python/NumPyVectors, matrices, gradients, distributions — and the tools to compute with them

What & why

Images are tensors; models are matrix multiplies; training is gradient descent; uncertainty is probability. You don't need a math degree, but you do need fluency with vectors/matrices, dot products, eigenvectors, gradients/chain rule, and basic probability. In parallel, get genuinely fast with Python + NumPy (vectorized array thinking) — it's the lingua franca of the whole field.

Learn

  • Linear algebra — matrix multiply, transpose/inverse, rank, eigen/SVD, why they matter geometrically.
  • Calculus — partial derivatives, the chain rule (this is backprop).
  • Probability — distributions, expectation/variance, Bayes' rule, Gaussians.
  • NumPy — broadcasting, indexing, vectorization instead of loops.
You've already touched this: the Kalman filter and Hungarian solver in your VisionTrack project are pure linear algebra + probability — revisit them once this clicks and they'll read like plain English.

Milestones

Resources

3Blue1Brown — Essence of Linear Algebra / Calculus (YouTube).Gilbert Strang — Linear Algebra (MIT OCW). NumPy official "absolute basics" guide.

01

Digital images & classical processing

Junior

Before deep learning, understand what an image is and the operations that shaped the field for 40 years — they still power preprocessing and augmentation today.

Pixels, colour spaces, filtering & convolutionHistograms, blur/sharpen, edges, morphology — the classical toolbox

What & why

An image is a grid of intensities (per channel). Convolution — sliding a small kernel over the image — is the single most important operation in vision; it does blurring, sharpening, and edge detection (Sobel/Canny), and it's literally what a CNN learns. Learn colour spaces (RGB/HSV/grayscale), histograms, thresholding, and morphology (erode/dilate) hands-on with OpenCV.

Learn

  • Convolution & kernels — the operation underneath everything.
  • Edges & gradients — Sobel, Canny; why gradients localize structure.
  • Colour spaces & histograms — HSV for colour, histogram equalization.
Connection: your colour-histogram appearance embedder in VisionTrack is exactly this classical toolbox used as a feature.

Milestones

Resources

Szeliski — "Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications" (free online; chs. 3–4).OpenCV-Python tutorials (official docs).

02

Features & multi-view geometry

Junior

How classical vision found and matched keypoints across images, and how cameras turn 3D into 2D. This is the backbone of SLAM, AR, and 3D reconstruction.

Keypoints (SIFT/ORB), matching, homography & epipolar geometryThe camera model, feature matching, and two-view geometry

What & why

Feature detectors/descriptors (SIFT, ORB) find repeatable keypoints and describe them so the same point can be matched across images — the basis of panorama stitching and SLAM. Multi-view geometry is the math of cameras: the pinhole model, intrinsics/extrinsics, calibration, homography, and the epipolar constraint that relates two views (and enables stereo depth).

Learn

  • Detectors & descriptors — Harris corners, SIFT/ORB, RANSAC for robust matching.
  • Camera model — projection, intrinsic K, distortion, calibration.
  • Two-view geometry — homography, fundamental/essential matrix, epipolar lines, stereo/triangulation.

Milestones

Resources

Hartley & Zisserman — "Multiple View Geometry" (the reference). Szeliski chs. 7–12.First Principles of Computer Vision (Shree Nayar, Columbia — YouTube series).

03

Machine learning fundamentals

Mid

The ideas underneath every model: learning from data, loss functions, optimization, and the bias/variance tug-of-war. Skip this and deep learning becomes cargo-cult.

Supervised learning, loss, gradient descent & generalizationTrain/val/test, overfitting, regularization, the bias–variance trade-off

What & why

A model is a function with parameters; training is minimizing a loss over data via gradient descent. The whole game is generalization — doing well on data you haven't seen. That means understanding overfitting/underfitting, regularization, the train/val/test split, and cross-validation. Start with linear/logistic regression before neural nets — every deep-learning concept is already here in miniature.

Learn

  • Loss functions — MSE, cross-entropy, and what they reward.
  • Gradient descent — learning rate, SGD vs batch, convergence.
  • Bias–variance — over/underfitting, regularization (L2, early stopping).
  • Evaluation — proper splits, why the test set is sacred.

Milestones

Resources

Andrew Ng — Machine Learning (Coursera/Stanford CS229 for depth).StatQuest (Josh Starmer, YouTube) for intuition.

04

Neural networks & backpropagation

Mid

The engine of modern CV. Build one from scratch once — hand-derive backprop — and you'll never be mystified by a framework again.

MLPs, activations, backprop & optimizersForward pass, the chain rule as backprop, SGD/Adam

What & why

A neural net stacks linear layers + non-linear activations (ReLU) so it can approximate complex functions. Backpropagation is just the chain rule applied efficiently to compute gradients of the loss w.r.t. every weight; optimizers (SGD, momentum, Adam) use those gradients to update. Understanding this cold is the single highest-leverage thing in the roadmap.

Learn

  • Forward pass — layers, weights, biases, activations.
  • Backprop — computational graph, chain rule, gradient flow.
  • Optimizers — SGD → momentum → Adam; learning-rate intuition.
You've already done this once: VisionTrack's RQ2 motion residual is a from-scratch NumPy MLP with hand-written backprop + Adam. Re-read residual.py after this stage — it's a perfect concrete example.

Milestones

Resources

Andrej Karpathy — "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" (micrograd → makemore).3Blue1Brown — Neural Networks series. Goodfellow et al. — "Deep Learning" book.

05

CNNs & training deep networks

Mid

Convolutional networks are how vision went deep. Learn the architecture ideas and the practical craft of actually getting a net to train.

Conv layers, classic architectures & the training craftPooling, receptive fields, ResNet, batchnorm, augmentation, transfer learning

What & why

A CNN reuses the same small kernels across the image (weight sharing) so it learns local patterns efficiently — edges → textures → parts → objects up the stack. Study the architecture lineage (LeNet → AlexNet → VGG → ResNet; skip connections are the key unlock) and the practical craft: data augmentation, batch normalization, dropout, learning-rate schedules, and transfer learning (fine-tuning a pretrained backbone — what you'll actually do 90% of the time).

Learn

  • Conv mechanics — kernels, stride, padding, pooling, receptive field.
  • Architectures — why ResNet's residual connections enabled very deep nets.
  • Training craft — augmentation, batchnorm, schedules, and how to debug a net that won't learn.
  • Transfer learning — feature extraction vs fine-tuning.

Milestones

Resources

Stanford CS231n — "CNNs for Visual Recognition" (the canonical course; notes are free).fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning. He et al. 2015 — "ResNet".

06

Image classification — the backbone task

Mid

The task that launched the deep-learning era (AlexNet, 2012). Everything else — detection, segmentation — is built on a classification backbone.

ImageNet, backbones & evaluationThe architecture lineage and top-1/top-5 accuracy

What & why

Classification = "what object is in this image?" ImageNet + AlexNet showed deep CNNs crush classical methods, kicking off the modern era. The evolution of backbones (VGG → ResNet → EfficientNet → ConvNeXt) is a story of accuracy vs compute; these pretrained backbones become the feature extractor for every downstream task, so knowing them is foundational.

Learn

  • The lineage — what each landmark architecture changed and why.
  • Metrics — top-1/top-5 accuracy, confusion matrices.
  • Backbone reuse — how classification pretraining transfers to detection/segmentation.

Milestones

Resources

Krizhevsky et al. 2012 — "AlexNet". Simonyan & Zisserman 2014 — "VGG".timm (PyTorch Image Models) — the practical backbone zoo.

07

Object detection

Mid

"What objects are where?" — boxes + labels. The most industrially useful CV task, and the input to your tracking work.

R-CNN → YOLO, anchors, NMS & mAPTwo-stage vs one-stage detectors and how detection is scored

What & why

Detection predicts a bounding box + class per object. Learn the two families: two-stage (R-CNN → Fast → Faster R-CNN: propose regions, then classify) and one-stage (YOLO, SSD, RetinaNet: predict directly, faster). Core mechanics that recur everywhere: anchors, IoU, non-max suppression (NMS), and the mAP metric. YOLO is what you'd deploy in practice.

Learn

  • Two-stage vs one-stage — the accuracy/speed trade-off.
  • Anchors & NMS — proposal generation and duplicate removal.
  • mAP — precision/recall curves, IoU thresholds, AP averaging.
Direct link: VisionTrack consumes detections as input. Running a real YOLOX detector on DanceTrack is one of your project's own "next steps" — this stage is the prerequisite for it.

Milestones

Resources

Ren et al. 2015 — "Faster R-CNN". Redmon et al. 2016 — "YOLO".Ultralytics YOLO docs (practical). Lin et al. 2017 — "Focal Loss / RetinaNet".

08

Segmentation

Mid

Pixel-level understanding: label every pixel (semantic) or every object's exact mask (instance). Powers medical imaging, autonomous driving, photo editing.

Semantic, instance & panoptic — FCN, U-Net, Mask R-CNNEncoder–decoder nets and per-pixel/per-mask prediction

What & why

Semantic segmentation classifies each pixel (road/car/person) — FCN, U-Net (encoder–decoder with skip connections), DeepLab (atrous convolutions). Instance segmentation separates individual objects with per-object masks — Mask R-CNN (detection + a mask head). Panoptic unifies both. U-Net in particular is everywhere (medical, diffusion models), so learn it well.

Learn

  • Encoder–decoder + skips — how U-Net recovers spatial detail.
  • Semantic vs instance vs panoptic — what each outputs.
  • Metrics — IoU / mean-IoU, Dice coefficient.

Milestones

Resources

Ronneberger et al. 2015 — "U-Net". He et al. 2017 — "Mask R-CNN".Kirillov et al. 2023 — "Segment Anything (SAM)" — the modern foundation model.

09

Embeddings, metric learning & tracking (video)

Mid

Moving from single images to identity and time: representations where "same thing" is close, and following objects across video frames.

Contrastive/triplet loss, re-ID, optical flow & multi-object trackingSimilarity embeddings, and the detect→predict→associate video loop

What & why

Metric learning trains embeddings so same-identity items are near and different ones far (triplet/contrastive loss) — the basis of face recognition and person re-ID. Video adds time: optical flow (per-pixel motion), action recognition, and multi-object tracking (MOT) — detect each frame, predict motion (Kalman), associate (Hungarian), manage identities (ByteTrack/DeepSORT).

Learn

  • Metric learning — triplet/contrastive loss, hard-negative mining, cosine similarity.
  • Tracking — Kalman + Hungarian + ByteTrack; ID switches, HOTA/IDF1 metrics.
  • Video representations — optical flow, temporal models.
This is your home turf. VisionTrack is a full from-scratch implementation of this entire stage — Kalman, Hungarian, ByteTrack, re-ID embeddings, HOTA/IDF1. If you can teach this stage from your own repo, you've mastered it.

Milestones

Resources

Schroff et al. 2015 — "FaceNet" (triplet loss). Wojke et al. 2017 — "DeepSORT".Zhang et al. 2022 — "ByteTrack". Luiten et al. 2021 — "HOTA".

10

Vision Transformers & attention

Senior

The architecture that took over. Attention replaced convolution as the default for large-scale vision — and unified vision with language.

Self-attention, ViT, DETR & SwinPatches as tokens, and detection/segmentation reframed with transformers

What & why

Self-attention lets every element attend to every other, capturing long-range relationships convolution struggles with. ViT chops an image into patches and treats them as tokens — with enough data it beats CNNs. DETR reframes detection as set prediction (no anchors, no NMS). Swin reintroduces hierarchy/locality for efficiency. Attention is also the bridge to multimodal models (next stage).

Learn

  • Attention — queries/keys/values, multi-head, positional encoding.
  • ViT — patch embedding, why it needs scale (data/pretraining).
  • Transformer detection — DETR's set prediction + bipartite matching (Hungarian again!).
Nice echo: DETR matches predictions to ground truth with the Hungarian algorithm — the exact solver you implemented in VisionTrack, now inside a transformer's loss.

Milestones

Resources

Vaswani et al. 2017 — "Attention Is All You Need". Dosovitskiy et al. 2020 — "ViT".Carion et al. 2020 — "DETR". Karpathy — "Let's build GPT" (attention, hands-on).

11

Self-supervised & multimodal learning

Senior

How to learn from unlabeled data, and how vision met language. This is where foundation models come from.

Contrastive (SimCLR/MoCo), masked (MAE) & CLIPPretext tasks, learned representations, and image–text alignment

What & why

Labels are expensive; self-supervised learning creates its own supervision. Contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo) pull augmentations of the same image together; masked methods (MAE) hide patches and reconstruct them. CLIP trains on image–text pairs so images and captions share an embedding space — enabling zero-shot classification and powering text-to-image generation. These pretrained representations are the "foundation" everything now fine-tunes from.

Learn

  • Contrastive learning — positive/negative pairs, InfoNCE loss.
  • Masked image modeling — MAE's reconstruction objective.
  • CLIP — joint image–text embedding, zero-shot transfer.

Milestones

Resources

Chen et al. 2020 — "SimCLR". He et al. 2021 — "MAE".Radford et al. 2021 — "CLIP".

12

Generative models — GANs → diffusion

Senior

Making images, not just reading them. Diffusion models power today's text-to-image systems; understand the lineage that got there.

VAEs, GANs & diffusion (DDPM → Stable Diffusion)Latent variables, adversarial training, and iterative denoising

What & why

Generative models learn the data distribution so they can sample new images. VAEs (encode to a latent, decode back), GANs (a generator vs a discriminator in a minimax game — sharp but unstable), and diffusion models (learn to reverse a gradual noising process — now state of the art). Latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion) runs the process in a compressed latent for efficiency; text conditioning via CLIP gives text-to-image.

Learn

  • VAE — latent space, the reparameterization trick.
  • GAN — adversarial loss, mode collapse, why training is fragile.
  • Diffusion — forward noising / learned reverse denoising; the U-Net backbone; conditioning.

Milestones

Resources

Goodfellow et al. 2014 — "GANs". Ho et al. 2020 — "DDPM".Rombach et al. 2022 — "Latent Diffusion / Stable Diffusion". Lil'Log (Weng) diffusion post.

13

3D vision & neural rendering

Senior

Recovering and representing the 3D world from images. Robotics, AR/VR, autonomous driving, and the NeRF/Gaussian-splatting wave.

Depth, point clouds, SfM/SLAM, NeRF & Gaussian splattingFrom 2D pixels to 3D geometry and novel-view synthesis

What & why

Builds on your Stage-02 geometry. Depth estimation (stereo or monocular), point clouds (and nets like PointNet), Structure-from-Motion / SLAM (reconstruct scene + camera path from many views). The modern wave is neural rendering: NeRF represents a scene as a continuous function you can render from any viewpoint; 3D Gaussian Splatting does it far faster. High-impact, geometry-heavy, and increasingly hot.

Learn

  • Depth & point clouds — stereo/mono depth, PointNet on unordered points.
  • SfM/SLAM — multi-view reconstruction + localization.
  • Neural rendering — NeRF's volumetric rendering; Gaussian splatting's speed win.

Milestones

Resources

Mildenhall et al. 2020 — "NeRF". Kerbl et al. 2023 — "3D Gaussian Splatting".Qi et al. 2017 — "PointNet".

14

Production CV, efficiency & MLOps

Senior

A model in a notebook isn't a product. Seniority is largely about shipping: making models fast, small, reliable, and monitored.

Deployment, ONNX/TensorRT, quantization, edge & monitoringFrom weights to a served, efficient, observable system

What & why

The gap between research and impact. Export (ONNX) and optimize (TensorRT, quantization to int8, pruning, distillation) to hit latency budgets; deploy to server or edge (mobile/embedded); build serving (batching, throughput/latency SLOs) and monitoring (data drift, performance regressions). This is also where reproducibility, versioning, and CI/CD for models live.

Learn

  • Model optimization — quantization, pruning, distillation; the accuracy/latency trade-off.
  • Runtimes — ONNX Runtime, TensorRT; CPU vs GPU vs edge.
  • MLOps — experiment tracking, model registry, monitoring/drift, CI for models.
You've practiced this: VisionTrack runs OSNet re-ID via ONNX Runtime on CPU, keeps a NumPy-only core with lazy heavy deps, hashes configs for reproducibility, and gates results in CI — all production-mindset patterns.

Milestones

Resources

Made With ML (Goku Mohandas) — MLOps course. ONNX Runtime / NVIDIA TensorRT docs.

15

Data, evaluation & experimental rigor

Senior

The unglamorous skills that separate credible results from noise — and the bridge into research.

Datasets, annotation, metrics, significance & reproducibilityMeasuring honestly — the difference between "looks better" and "is better"

What & why

Models are only as good as the data and the yardstick. Learn to build/clean datasets, handle class imbalance and label noise, choose the right metric for the task, and — crucially — report results with seed variance and significance tests, not a single lucky number. Reproducibility (fixed seeds, config hashing, cached artifacts) is what makes a result trustworthy.

Learn

  • Metrics per task — accuracy/mAP/IoU/HOTA and their blind spots.
  • Statistical rigor — mean±std, paired tests, bootstrap CIs, effect size.
  • Reproducibility — seeds, config hashing, ablations that isolate one variable.
This is literally what VisionTrack demonstrates. Its whole thesis is honest measurement — paired Wilcoxon tests, bootstrap CIs, config hashes, and two reported "honest negatives". That's a portfolio-grade example of this stage.

Milestones

Resources

Your own VisionTrack eval/stats.py + the study-guide's rigor stage. Papers-with-Code for benchmark norms.

16

Reading & reproducing papers

Research

The research on-ramp. Before you can create new knowledge, you must be able to absorb and verify existing work fluently.

How to read a paper, and reproduce its resultThe three-pass method, then re-implement and match the numbers

What & why

Research literacy is a trainable skill. Use the three-pass method (skim → grasp → deeply verify), keep a literature log, and — the real level-up — reproduce a paper's headline result from scratch. Reproducing forces you to confront the details papers gloss over and is the single best bridge from "consumer" to "producer" of research.

Learn

  • Reading strategy — three passes; identify the one core contribution.
  • Reproduction — re-implement, match reported numbers, note the gaps.
  • Critical reading — spot weak baselines, cherry-picked metrics, missing ablations.

Milestones

Resources

Keshav — "How to Read a Paper" (the three-pass method). Papers with Code, arXiv, Yannic Kilcher paper walkthroughs.

17

Doing original research

Research

Producing new knowledge: forming a falsifiable question, designing clean experiments, and communicating the result honestly.

Hypotheses, experimental design, writing & peer reviewFrom a question to a defensible, communicated contribution

What & why

Research is a loop: find a gap, form a falsifiable hypothesis, design controlled experiments that isolate the variable, run them with statistical rigor, and write it up so others can verify. A well-measured negative result is a contribution. Learn the norms: strong baselines, honest ablations, clear limitations, reproducible code — and how peer review works.

Learn

  • Framing — a falsifiable question with a predicted outcome, not "let's try X".
  • Design — controls, ablations, confounds, significance.
  • Communication — paper structure (abstract→method→results→limitations), figures, a clear story.
You already did a mini version of this. VisionTrack is a controlled study with falsifiable RQs, paired significance tests, honest negatives, and a mini-paper README. Turning it into a short workshop paper or blog write-up is a concrete Stage-17 exercise.

Milestones

Resources

Michael Nielsen — "Principles of Effective Research". Distill.pub for exemplary communication. University "how to do research" guides.

18

Frontier & open problems

Research

Where the field is going. Pick a thread, go deep, and contribute — this is a moving target, so learning-to-learn matters more than any single topic.

Foundation models, VLMs, embodied & robust visionOpen directions: generalist models, video understanding, reliability

What & why

Active frontiers as of the mid-2020s: vision-language models (VLMs) that reason over images and text; promptable foundation models (SAM-style) that generalize across tasks; video & long-horizon understanding; embodied/robotic vision; efficiency (doing more with less compute); and robustness, safety, and fairness (distribution shift, adversarial examples, bias). The meta-skill is staying current and picking a niche to own.

Learn / do

  • Pick one thread and read its last ~2 years of key papers.
  • Follow the field — arXiv, top venues (CVPR/ICCV/ECCV/NeurIPS), strong newsletters/blogs.
  • Contribute — open-source a reproduction, write a survey blog, or propose a small novel study.

Milestones

Resources

CVPR/ICCV/ECCV/NeurIPS proceedings. arxiv-sanity, Papers with Code trending, The Batch (deeplearning.ai).
