H2.1 · the tracker on real footage

Same tracker, real streets.

The demo you can play with is synthetic. This is the identical pipeline — YOLOX detections associated by the from-scratch 8-state Kalman filter, Hungarian matching, and ByteTrack — run over a real, crowded intersection. No tracking library: the association, gating, and lifecycle are all NumPy.

How it was made

Every frame is detected by a COCO YOLOX-nano ONNX model, filtered to the common street classes, then handed to the from-scratch tracker. Because a COCO detector scores lower than a MOT-tuned one, the spawn/confirm gates are relaxed a touch — otherwise the exact same code path as the benchmark study. The whole thing is one command:

# 1. a YOLOX-nano ONNX model (see the docs) + 2. any licence-clean clip
python scripts/render_video_demo.py clip.mp4 tracked.mp4 \
    --model models/yolox_nano.onnx --max-frames 300

Source: “Bangkok Traffic During Enduring Partners 26” by SSgt James Bunn, U.S. Army National Guard — public domain (a U.S. federal work), so the annotated result is freely redistributable. The model and raw footage stay out of the repo (weight- and imagery-clean); the script and tests are what live in git. Full recipe in the documentation.
