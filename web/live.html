How it works

Each frame follows the exact tracking-by-detection flow the study benchmarks, just moved into the browser:

  1. Detect — a COCO-SSD model (TensorFlow.js, WebGL) returns candidate boxes with scores. This is the only learned, imported component.
  2. Predict — every existing track advances one step through its 8-state constant-velocity Kalman filter (position + aspect + height, and their velocities).
  3. Associate — ByteTrack's two rounds: high-score detections are matched to tracks by IoU via the Hungarian solver, then leftover tracks get a second chance against the low-score band — the trick that recovers objects through momentary occlusion.
  4. Manage — a tentative → confirmed → deleted lifecycle spawns new ids only after a few consistent hits and retires tracks that go unseen, so the id you see is a committed one.

Steps 2–4 are the from-scratch tracker, re-implemented line-for-line from the Python core in the write-up — no tracking library on either side. Frame rate depends on your device's GPU; the detector, not the tracker, is the bottleneck (the same finding as the real-footage and profiling results).
